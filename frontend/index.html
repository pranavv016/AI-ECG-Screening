Educational Information

What is ECG screening?

An electrocardiogram (ECG) records the electrical activity of the heart over time using electrodes placed on the skin. Each heartbeat produces a characteristic waveform — the P wave, QRS complex, and T wave — that reflects the timing of electrical signals through the atria and ventricles.

Automated ECG screening tools attempt to identify patterns in this waveform, such as heart rate, rhythm regularity, and waveform shape, that may be associated with certain cardiac conditions. In research settings, machine learning models are trained on large labeled datasets to recognize these patterns.

Limitations of this prototype

  • Part 5 adds a supervised Random Forest benchmark for NORMAL vs ABNORMAL classification of 187-sample MIT-BIH-derived heartbeat segments.
  • Even a completed version would not replace interpretation by a qualified clinician.
  • ECG interpretation software can be affected by noise, electrode placement, and signal artifacts.
  • This tool has not been validated, tested, or approved for any clinical or diagnostic use.
  • Results from prototype systems like this should never inform real medical decisions.
